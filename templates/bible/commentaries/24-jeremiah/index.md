## Chapter 1

### Date: January 12, 2026

- (v. 1) 1 These are the words of Jeremiah son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth in the territory of Benjamin.
  - Priest residing in Benjamin, in Judah, after the split of the kingdoms, for Levi has no land
- (vv. 2-3) 2 The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah in the thirteenth year of the reign of Josiah son of Amon king of Judah,
  3 and through the days of Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah, until the fifth month of the eleventh year of Zedekiah
  son of Josiah king of Judah, when the people of Jerusalem went into exile.
  - Jeremiah serves under 4 kings of Israel
  - He is the one to lament the final good king of Israel/Judah
- (v. 5) 5 Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I set you apart and appointed you as a
  prophet to the nations.
  - Not that all children are known by God in a regenerating sense
- (v. 9) 9 Then the LORD reached out His hand, touched my mouth, and said to me: “Behold, I have put My words in your mouth.
  - Parallels Isaiah 6
- (vv. 9-10) 9 Then the LORD reached out His hand, touched my mouth, and said to me: “Behold, I have put My words in your
  mouth. 10 See, I have appointed you today over nations and kingdoms to uproot and tear down, to destroy and overthrow,
  to build and plant.”
  - The prophet Jeremiah is placed over all nations so that he may provide the message of the New Covenant to all nations,
    it is not only to Judah
- (v. 10) 10See, I have appointed you today over nations and kingdoms to uproot and tear down, to destroy and overthrow,
  to build and plant.”
  - What is currently present must be taken away before the new can come
  - The Old Covenant must fade away for the New Covenant to replace it
- (v. 11) 11 And the word of the Lord came to me, saying, “Jeremiah, what do you see?” And I said, “I see an almond branch.”
  - Almond sounds similar to “watching” and tells Jeremiah to pay close attention to what comes next
- (v. 13) 13 The word of the Lord came to me a second time, saying, “What do you see?” And I said, “I see a boiling pot,
  facing away from the north.”
  - Boiling pot signifies judgment, and this judgment is directed toward the Northern kingdom and those who sin in the like
- (vv. 15-16) 15 For behold, I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the Lord, and they shall
  come, and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls all around and
  against all the cities of Judah. 16 And I will declare my judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking me.
  They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands.
  - Multiple kingdoms will come and ransack the nation of Judah: Chaldeans and Assyrians in particular
- (vv. 17-19) 17 But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed
  by them, lest I dismay you before them. 18 And I, behold, I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze
  walls, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. 19
  They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the Lord, to deliver
  you.”
  - The promise of salvation amongst God’s people is only given to Jeremiah
    - There is no defense of man that will protect us the same way the LORD does
- (v. 19) 19 They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the Lord,
  to deliver you.”
  - Jeremiah will succeed in doing the work the Lord is doing to deliver His people

## Chapter 2

### Date: January 19, 2026

- (v. 2) 2 “Go and proclaim in the hearing of Jerusalem that this is what the LORD says: ‘I remember the devotion of your
  youth, your love as a bride, how you followed Me in the wilderness, in a land not sown.
  - Israel was more devoted, relative to how they are now to Jeremiah
  - There is some sense of exaggeration here, to make Israel recognize the way that their childlike faith was desirable
    to the Lord
    - We may have a kind of fervor in our early converted years that we should be encouraged by
  - I feel I was being guided through the wilderness in my childhood ([Talking about bicycles \- C.S. Lewis](https://matiane.wordpress.com/2021/09/11/talking-about-bicycles-by-c-s-lewis/))
- (v. 3) 3 Israel was holy to the LORD, the firstfruits of His harvest. All who devoured her were found guilty; disaster
  came upon them,’” declares the LORD.
  - The converted are the firstfruits of God’s creatures
  - Balaam could not even utter a curse against Israel
- (v. 4) Hear the word of the LORD, O house of Jacob, and all you families of the house of Israel.
  - Why does God use “Jacob” and Israel in the same verse?
    - Consider the names themselves and their meanings. Is God segmenting the elect from the non-elect?
- (v. 5) 5 This is what the LORD says: “What fault did your fathers find in Me that they strayed so far from Me? They followed
  worthless idols, and became worthless themselves.
  - Those who worship become as the idols, worthless, dumb, and blind
  - Dumb/mute is important because God is the word (John 1:1)
- (v. 6) 6 They did not ask, ‘Where is the LORD who brought us up from the land of Egypt, who led us through the wilderness,
  through a land of deserts and pits, a land of drought and darkness, a land where no one travels and no one lives?’
  - Each of the salvific signs in the desert are typological of Christ, and they continue to forget them
  - Even when they recognize the Lord, they did so with complaining
- (v. 7) 7 I brought you into a fertile land to eat its fruit and bounty, but you came and defiled My land and made My
  inheritance detestable.
  - Psalm 106:38 “the land was polluted with blood”
  - God has not yet fully removed His presence from the temple, but is beginning to remove this
- (v. 8) 8 Your priests did not ask, ‘Where is the Lord?’ Those responsible for teaching my law did not really know me.
  Your rulers rebelled against me. Your prophets prophesied in the name of the god Baal. They all worshiped idols that
  could not help them.
  - 1 Samuel 2:12-17 17 Thus the sin of these young men was severe in the sight of the LORD, for they were treating the
    LORD’s offering with contempt.
  - I live surrounded by idols
- (v. 9) 9 “So, once more I will state my case against you,” says the Lord. “I will also state it against your children
  and grandchildren.
  - We should not put God on the dock/witness stand
    - Consider how Job did sin in this way
    - Israel does wrestle with the Lord
- (v. 13) 13 “For My people have committed two evils: They have forsaken Me, the fountain of living water, and they have
  dug their own cisterns—broken cisterns that cannot hold water.
  - Fountain vs cistern
    - One is constant and active provision
    - One is stored up and goes stale
- (v. 14) 14 Is Israel a slave? Was he born into slavery? Why then has he become prey?
  - God expects them to answer “no” to the first two questions
  - Then to respond back by asking them why they think they are in their current state
  - John 8:31-38
- (v. 17) 17 Have you not brought this on yourself by forsaking the LORD your God when He led you in the way?
  - Why is Israel responsible if God has ordained this to pass?
    - [https://www.apologeticscentral.org/post/the-privation-theory-of-evil-and-the-donum-superadditum](https://www.apologeticscentral.org/post/the-privation-theory-of-evil-and-the-donum-superadditum)
    - "Anything short of referring the justification of the existence of evil to the character of God and His purpose to
      glorify Himself, and to His sovereign will to accomplish this by means of creation and sin, is unsatisfactory. Anything
      short of this is illogical and unbiblical."
- (v. 19) 19 Your own evil will discipline you; your own apostasies will reprimand you. Consider and realize how evil and
  bitter it is for you to forsake the LORD your God and to have no fear of Me,”
  - Is this referring to Christ?
    - _Yahweh, the God of Armies_
- (vv. 20, 33) 20 “For long ago you broke your yoke and tore off your chains, saying, ‘I will not serve!’ Indeed, on every
  high hill and under every green tree you lay down as a prostitute. 33 How skillfully you pursue love! Even the most immoral
  of women could learn from your ways.
  - Hosea and Ezekiel echo these sentiments
- (v. 21) 21 I had planted you like a choice vine from the very best seed. How could you turn yourself before Me into a
  rotten, wild vine?
  - Christians are recognized by the fruit they bear on the vine of Christ
- (vv. 22-23) 22 Although you wash with lye and use an abundance of soap, the stain of your guilt is still before Me,”
  23 “How can you say, ‘I am not defiled; I have not run after the Baals’? Look at your behavior in the valley; acknowledge
  what you have done. You are a swift young she-camel galloping here and there,
  - God does not give a way to escape the accusation, but rather asks them to rhetorically consider
- (v. 27) 27 say to a tree, ‘You are my father,’ and to a stone, ‘You gave me birth.’ They have turned their backs to Me
  and not their faces. Yet in the time of trouble, they say, ‘Rise up and save us!’
  - Continual mocking of idols
  - Israel has many many idols and are dedicated to their betrayal
  - If they could find something other than God to devote themselves to, they did
- (v. 30) 30 “I have struck your sons in vain; they accepted no discipline. Your own sword has devoured your prophets like
  a voracious lion.”
  - Reminds me of Eli and his children’s failures
  - There are multiple destructions of the priesthoods from Samuel
- (v. 35) 35 you say, ‘I am innocent. Surely His anger will turn from me.’ Behold, I will judge you, because you say,
  ‘I have not sinned.’
  - Israel fails to rest on the Lord for salvation and comfort
  - They instead go to those around them who have rejected the Lord.

## Chapter 3

### Date: February 9, 2026

- (v. 1, 5) 1 “If a man divorces his wife and she leaves him to marry another, can he ever return to her? Would not such
  a land be completely defiled? But you have played the harlot with many lovers—and you would return to Me?” declares the
  LORD. 5 Will He be angry forever? Will He be indignant to the end?’ This you have spoken, but you keep doing all the evil
  you can.”
  - “If you could do more evil, you would.
- (v. 2) 2 “Lift up your eyes to the barren heights and see. Is there any place where you have not been violated? You sat
  beside the highways waiting for your lovers, like a nomad in the desert. You have defiled the land with your prostitution
  and wickedness.
  - God holds all accountable to what they have done
- (v. 7) 7 Yet even after she had done all that, I thought that she might come back to me. But she did not. Her sister,
  unfaithful Judah, saw what she did.
  - God wishes for and expects repentance from His people.
  - God chooses Judah to bear the Christ, despite them being the least.
- (v. 11) 11 Then the Lord said to me, “Under the circumstances, wayward Israel could even be considered less guilty than
  unfaithful Judah.
  - Seeing the sin of one and going forth in committing that sin is worse.
- (v. 12-13) 12 Go, proclaim this message toward the north: ‘Return, O faithless Israel,’ declares the LORD. ‘I will no
  longer look on you with anger, for I am merciful,’ declares the LORD. ‘I will not be angry forever. 13 Only acknowledge
  your guilt, that you have rebelled against the LORD your God. You have scattered your favors to foreign gods under every
  green tree and have not obeyed My voice,’” declares the LORD.
- (v. 14) 14 “Return, O faithless children,” declares the LORD, “for I am your master, and I will take you—one from a city
  and two from a family—and bring you to Zion.
  - Hebrews 12:22-24 Zion is the heavenly Jerusalem.
  - The promises of Zion given here are to all of God’s elect, bought by Christ’s blood.
- (v. 16-17) 16 “In those days, when you multiply and increase in the land,” declares the LORD, “they will no longer discuss
  the ark of the covenant of the LORD. It will never come to mind, and no one will remember it or miss it, nor will another
  one be made. 17 At that time they will call Jerusalem The Throne of the LORD, and all the nations will be gathered in
  Jerusalem to honor the name of the LORD. They will no longer follow the stubbornness of their evil hearts.
  - Promises are made to Jerusalem, which is the church
- (v. 18) 18 In those days the house of Judah will walk with the house of Israel, and they will come together from the land
  of the north to the land that I gave to your fathers as an inheritance.
  - Hebrews 11:8-10
- (vv. 19-25) God desires for us to have repentance for our misguided worship, and to desire sonship with Him.
  - We are to not desire anything but the promises He has given.

## Chapter 5

### Date: March 2, 2026

- (v. 1) 1 “Go up and down the streets of Jerusalem. Look now and take note; search her squares. If you can find a single
  person, anyone who acts justly, anyone who seeks the truth, then I will forgive the city.
  - “Squares” meaning “public places”, so could you pluck a “public” person?
  - Should remind us of Sodom and Gomorrah.
- (v. 2) 2 Although they say, ‘As surely as the LORD lives,’ they are swearing falsely.”
  - Reminds me of the false swearing of the Pharisees, or their “swearing upon the gold of the temple”
- (v. 3) 3 O LORD, do not Your eyes look for truth? You struck them, but they felt no pain. You finished them off, but they
  refused to accept discipline. They have made their faces harder than stone and refused to repent.
  - The Second London Baptist Confession of Faith, 5.5 The most wise, righteous, and gracious God doth often times leave
    for a season His own children to manifold temptations and the corruptions of their own hearts, to chastise them for
    their former sins, or to discover unto them the hidden strength of corruption and deceitfulness of their hearts, that
    they may be humbled; and to raise them to a more close and constant dependence for their support upon Himself; and to
    make them more watchful against all future occasions of sin, and for other just and holy ends.⁠¹⁠ So that whatsoever
    befalls any of His elect is by His appointment, for His glory, and their good.⁠²⁠
- (v. 4-5) 4 Then I said, “They are only the poor; they have played the fool, for they do not know the way of the LORD,
  the justice of their God. 5 I will go to the powerful and speak to them. Surely they know the way of the LORD, the justice
  of their God.”
  - Makes it seem as if verse 1 is referring to the masses, not just the elite.
  - For verse 5, the masses don’t understand, but the elites “should” know better.
    - Therefore, they are more at fault.
    - We, by being Americans, are by nature the elite.
- (v. 6) 6 Therefore a lion from the forest will strike them down, a wolf from the desert will ravage them. A leopard will
  lie in wait near their cities, and everyone who ventures out will be torn to pieces. For their rebellious acts are many,
  and their unfaithful deeds are numerous.
  - Their sins are many, not one that the Lord is responding to
  - The Lord is right to reject His people even after one sin, but He is merciful and has dealt with them a long time.
- (v. 7-11) 7 “Why should I forgive you? Your children have forsaken Me and sworn by gods that are not gods. I satisfied
  their needs, yet they committed adultery and assembled at the houses of prostitutes. 8 They are well-fed, lusty stallions,
  each neighing after his neighbor’s wife. 9 Should I not punish them for these things?” declares the LORD. “Should I not
  avenge Myself on such a nation as this? 10 Go up through her vineyards and ravage them, but do not finish them off. Strip
  off her branches, for they do not belong to the LORD. 11 For the house of Israel and the house of Judah have been utterly
  unfaithful to Me,” declares the LORD.
  - This is fulfilled by the Babylonian sacking of Israel.
  - (v. 10) God will not fully destroy Israel, for He still has plans to save them through Christ, the Messiah.
- (v. 12-13) 12 They have lied about the LORD and said: “He will not do anything; harm will not come to us; we will not
  see sword or famine. 13 The prophets are but wind, for the word is not in them. So let their own predictions befall them.”
  - Reminds me of a Messianic Jew who spoke to me and told me how he and other Messianic Jews are in a state of constant
    fear about what the Lord may inflict upon them.
  - (v. 13) The prophets being “but wind” is a double entendre for calling them “windbags” while incidentally identifying
    them as having the “wind” of the Holy Spirit.
  - 1 Kings 22:15

## Chapter 6

### Date: March 16, 2026

- (v. 1) 1 Run for cover, O sons of Benjamin; flee from Jerusalem! Sound the ram’s horn in Tekoa; send up a signal over
  Beth-haccherem, for disaster looms from the north, even great destruction.
  - God gives a warning to His people that, if they listen to Him now, they will be preserved.
- (v. 2) 2 Though she is beautiful and delicate, I will destroy the Daughter of Zion.
  - God likens His people to a beautiful maiden, reinforcing that He does love His people.
- (v. 4-5) 4 “Prepare for battle against her; rise up, let us attack at noon. Woe to us, for the daylight is fading; the
  evening shadows grow long. 5 Rise up, let us attack by night and destroy her fortresses!”
  - Despite losing time, the enemy is so desirous to destroy Jerusalem that they will not wait for the next day but will
    go up at night.
- (v. 7) 7 As a well gushes its water, so she pours out her evil. Violence and destruction resound in her; sickness and
  wounds are ever before Me.
  - There is nearly no difference in Jerusalem if it gets sacked or not, for it is already occupied by the enemy of sin.
  - In a similar way as we need to put sin to death, the city must be destroyed as our bodies must be destroyed to be restored.
- (v. 8) 8 Be forewarned, O Jerusalem, or I will turn away from you; I will make you a desolation, a land without inhabitant.
  - God pleads again with His people that they turn from their sin.
- (v. 10) 10 I answered, “Who would listen if I spoke to them and warned them? Their ears are so closed that they cannot
  hear! Indeed, the Lord’s message is offensive to them. They do not like it at all.
  - Reminds of Isaiah, where the people are blind and deaf like their idols.
  - Our world now fits into verse 10, where the Lord’s message is offensive to them.
- (v. 12) 12 Their houses will be turned over to others, their fields and wives as well, for I will stretch out My hand
  against the inhabitants of the land,” declares the LORD.
  - Verbatim curses from Deuteronomy.
- (v. 15) 15 Are they ashamed of the abomination they have committed? No, they have no shame at all; they do not even know
  how to blush. So they will fall among the fallen; when I punish them, they will collapse,” says the LORD.
  - Shame is real and we should know how to blush.
  - We should know how to accept the Lord’s discipline and not collapse.
- (v. 16) 16 This is what the LORD says: “Stand at the crossroads and look. Ask for the ancient paths: ‘Where is the good
  way?’ Then walk in it, and you will find rest for your souls. But they said, ‘We will not walk in it!’
  - Should be the slogan verse for the paleoconservative.
- (v. 18) 18 Therefore hear, O nations, and learn, O congregations, what will happen to them.
  - One of the occasions where the Lord warns non-Israel of their actions.
- (v. 20) 20 What use to Me is frankincense from Sheba or sweet cane from a distant land? Your burnt offerings are not
  acceptable; your sacrifices do not please Me.”
  - Warning the nations to not continue to lure God’s people into false worship, and to not perform false worship themselves.
- (v. 21) 21 Therefore this is what the LORD says: “I will lay stumbling blocks before this people; fathers and sons alike
  will be staggered; friends and neighbors will perish.”
  - Isaiah 8:14 Christ himself will be a stumbling block to Israel
- (v. 27) 27 “I have appointed you to examine My people like ore, so you may know and try their ways.
  - Interesting passage for word study, as translations differ greatly in how they translate this

## Chapter 7

### Date: March 23, 2026

- (v. 3) 3 Thus says the LORD of Hosts, the God of Israel: Correct your ways and deeds, and I will let you live in this
  place
  - God is still asking them to repent
- (v. 4) 4 Do not trust in deceptive words, saying: ‘This is the temple of the LORD, the temple of the LORD, the temple
  of the LORD.’
  - The three mentions here could be in relation to the three times Jews would return
  - The three mentions could also demonstrate the completeness of their trust in the “temple” rather than the LORD
  - When the LORD lets them be ransacked, he explicitly does not prevent the Ark from being taken and the temple destroyed,
    to demonstrate how foolish this notion was.
- (v. 10-11) 10 and then come and stand before Me in this house, which bears My Name, and say, ‘We are delivered, so we
  can continue with all these abominations’? 11 Has this house, which bears My Name, become a den of robbers in your sight?
  Yes, I too have seen it, declares the LORD.
  - Christ quotes then when He clears out the temple. (σπήλαιον σπήλαιον)
  - God again points out to them that them being in the temple will not save them.
- (v. 12) 12 But go now to the place in Shiloh where I first made a dwelling for My Name, and see what I did to it because
  of the wickedness of My people Israel.
  - In the same way that, in the past, the Ark was taken from Shiloh and the “LORD’s presence left Israel”, the LORD will
    leave them again.
- (v. 16) 16 As for you, do not pray for these people, do not offer a plea or petition on their behalf, and do not beg Me,
  for I will not listen to you.
  - Do not pray for all people
- (v. 23) 23 but this is what I commanded them: Obey Me, and I will be your God, and you will be My people. You must walk
  in all the ways I have commanded you, so that it may go well with you.
  - The LORD does not only care about how they do sacrifice, but that they obey him generally.

## Chapter 8

### Date: March 30, 2026

- (v. 2) 2 They will be exposed to the sun and moon, and to all the host of heaven which they have loved, served, followed,
  consulted, and worshiped. Their bones will not be gathered up or buried, but will become like dung lying on the ground.
  - It is almost a full reversal of the covenant made with Abraham, especially if you have a two-covenant view of what happens
    with Abraham, where this is a reversal of the land covenant.
- (v. 10) 10 Therefore I will give their wives to other men and their fields to new owners. For from the least of them to
  the greatest, all are greedy for gain; from prophet to priest, all practice deceit.
  - Inverse of God giving to them of what they have not earned, and instead taking it all away.
- (v. 4-13) God is reiterating what he has already said throughout the whole book
- (v. 14-15) 14 Why are we just sitting here? Gather together, let us flee to the fortified cities and perish there, for
  the LORD our God has doomed us. He has given us poisoned water to drink, because we have sinned against the LORD. 15 We
  hoped for peace, but no good has come, for a time of healing, but there was only terror.
  - Hyper-Calvinism present in the Old Testament.
- (v. 22) 22 Is there no balm in Gilead? Is no physician there? Why then has the health of the daughter of my people not
  been restored?
  - The spiritual state of all who deny Christ. There is healing available, yet they do not receive it.
