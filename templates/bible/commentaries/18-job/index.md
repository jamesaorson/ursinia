## Chapter 1

### November 16, 2025

- (v. 7) “Where have you come from?” said the Lord to Satan. “From roaming through the earth,” he replied, “and walking
  back and forth in it.”
  - God speaks condescendingly to Satan, as He does to us, with questions
  - God speaks to created beings in this manner across the board
- (v. 8) Then the Lord said to Satan, “Have you considered My servant Job? For there is no one on earth like him, a man
  who is blameless and upright, who fears God and shuns evil.”
  - I have often heard atheists question why God would bother to say this to Satan, as if He is acting out of pride, but
    God is rather fully aware of what will occur and provokes Satan into his sin in order that He may discipline Job and
    bring Job to deeper truths and understanding of God.
- (v. 16) “While he was still speaking, another messenger came and reported: “The fire of God fell from heaven. It burned
  and consumed the sheep and the servants, and I alone have escaped to tell you!”
  - Two possibilities here:
    1. The fire of God is actually from God, and He is orchestrating the evils that Satan is doing (unlikely)
    2. We are being shown how man can misinterpret the actor behind these matters
  - Regardless, Job worships (v. 20)

## Chapter 2

### November 17, 2025

- (v. 10) “You speak as a foolish woman speaks,” he told her. “Should we accept from God only good and not adversity?” In
  all this, Job did not sin in what he said.
  - Job does not understand that God can use adversity for teaching/disciplining as well
  - Job sees God as some kind of full agent who can do as He pleases, with no real need to be held accountable to anyone
    else for He is above all and over all

## Chapter 3

### November 18, 2025

- (v. 24) I sigh when food is put before me, and my groans pour out like water.
  - I would venture to say that, at this point, Job has begun to sin against the Lord with speech, however, the thing that
    Satan believed would occur was that Job would reject the Lord, not just sin against Him.
  - It is important for ourselves to recognize this: that sin is not inherently rejection of the Lord, but rejection of
    the Lord is inherently sinful.
  - He rejects now even the good, (v. 25) For the thing I feared has overtaken me, and what I dreaded has befallen me.

## Chapter 4

### November 19, 2025

- (v. 3-4) Surely you have instructed many, and have strengthened their feeble hands. Your words have steadied those who
  stumbled; you have braced the knees that were buckling.
  - Job, being the greatest man in the East, was one who strengthened other people, not simply overtaking them.
  - A great man will support those below him, and his riches and wisdom outpour onto others.
- (v. 7) Consider now, I plead: Who, being innocent, has ever perished? Or where have the upright been destroyed?
  - The real answers to these are
    - Everyone
    - For all of time
  - Answered by v. 17-21 ‘Can a mortal be more righteous than God, or a man more pure than his Maker? If God puts no trust
    in His servants, and He charges His angels with error, how much more those who dwell in houses of clay, whose foundations
    are in the dust, who can be crushed like a moth! They are smashed to pieces from dawn to dusk; unnoticed, they perish
    forever. Are not their tent cords pulled up, so that they die without wisdom?’
    - Whether or not Eliphaz realizes he has answered his prior rhetorical question is probably up for discussion. To me,
      it seems that he does not believe that God takes His full authority to the extent which the righteous also may suffer
      under His judgment.
    - Rather we know that God both has the right and the reason to do so, including to discipline his elect.
