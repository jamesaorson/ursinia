1. Samuel dies and all Israel mourns him ([v. 1](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-1)).
1. The narrator introduces Nabal and Abigail before the conflict begins
   ([vv. 2](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-2)–[3](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-3)).
   - Nabal, important to know that his name means "fool".
   - Abigail is discerning and beautiful.
1. David sends ten men to Nabal request
   ([vv. 4](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-4)–[9](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-9)).
   - Sheep-shearing was a time of celebration, feasting, and generosity.
   - David also frames his request around service rendered: his men had been a wall of protection around Nabal's shepherds in the wilderness, like a proper king.
1. Nabal responds with contempt.
   - He compares David to a runaway slave ([v. 10](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-10)), which may also be a
     subtle jab at David having been Saul’s servant.
   - He will not give what is customary to men he claims not to know, but he obviously knows who David is.
1. David arms four hundred men and rides to destroy Nabal's household
   ([vv. 12](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-12)–[13](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-13)).
   - The insult strikes at David's identity and his men's honor.
   - David mistakenly takes matters into his own hands without first inquiring of the Lord.
1. Abigail acts without hesitation and without telling Nabal
   ([vv. 14](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-14)–[19](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-19)).
   - One of Nabal's servants brings her the report and names Nabal as the one at fault, saying that plainly, "he is such
     a worthless man that one cannot speak to him" ([v. 17](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-17)).
   - Abigail loads up tribute and goes to intercept David.
1. Abigail's speech is the theological center of the chapter
   ([vv. 24](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-24)–[31](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-31)), reminding David
   that the king is to inquire of the Lord and not waste himself on worthless men.
   - She calls Nabal by his name and nature ([v. 25](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-25)).
   - She tells David that the Lord will make him a sure house, and that the Lord will deal well with him, if only he
     will remember His promises ([v. 31](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-31)).
1. David blesses Abigail and accepts her restraint
   ([vv. 32](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-32)–[35](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-35)), realizing the
   Lord has restrained him from sin yet again.
   - The Lord demonstrates His justice by dealing with Nabal Himself
     ([vv. 36](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-36)–[38](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-38)).
   - David did not need to act.
   - The LORD avenged the insult without David incurring bloodguilt.
1. We receive an update on David’s wives.
   - David takes Abigail as his wife, along with Ahinoam.
   - Saul has given Michal to another man
     ([vv. 39](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-39)–[44](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-25-44)), showing he has
     begun to work against David again.
