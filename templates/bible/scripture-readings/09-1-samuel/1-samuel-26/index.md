1. The story sounds similar to David and Saul in the cave.
   - This is the same group that betrayed David in [ch. 23](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23), and Saul is camped near the same hill of Hachilah.
   - We are about to see whether David has learned anything, and whether Saul has.
1. Abishai reads David having an opportunity over Saul as divine appointment, exactly as David's men did in the cave
   ([v. 8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-8); [cf. 24:4](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-24-4)).
   - The temptation is the same as before, but more intense, for they are in Saul’s camp.
   - David refuses on the same grounds as before: that the LORD's anointed may not be destroyed by a man's hand
   ([v. 9](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-9)).
   - David names three possible ends for Saul, and none of which are by David’s hand. ([v. 10](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-10)).
1. David uses the objects he took from Saul to rebuke Abner for failing to protect Saul, and to prove to Saul David’s loyalty
   ([vv. 14](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-14)–[21](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-21)).
1. David reminds them that the LORD renders to every man his righteousness and faithfulness ([vv. 23](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-23)–[24](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-24)).
1. Saul blesses David and predicts his success ([v. 25](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-26-25)), then they each go their own way.
   - Saul’s response is more muted than in [ch. 24](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-24), for there is no weeping, no covenant, just a nearly bare recognition from Saul.
   - The gap between David and Saul as men capable of leading the nation continues to widen.
