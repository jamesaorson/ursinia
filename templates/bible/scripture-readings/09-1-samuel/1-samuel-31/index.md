1. Israel is reported to have fallen and its soldiers effectively given up.
   - The battle is reported unceremoniously, for we have already been told this would happen in ([ch. 28](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-19)).
1. The Philistines kill Saul's sons: Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua
   ([v. 2](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-2)).
   - Saul’s house is under judgment.
   - The covenant Jonathan made with David in [ch. 20](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-20) now
     binds David to Jonathan's offspring, which will come into play in the next book.
1. Sau asks his armor-bearer to kill him
   ([vv. 3](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-3)–[4](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-4)).
   - He is afraid that the uncircumcised will make sport of him, so his last concern is his
     own shame rather than his people or his God.
   - The armor-bearer will not do it, so Saul falls on his own sword, killing himself, still trying to control his own fate.
1. Israel demanded a king, and the book closes with that king dead and the land occupied.
   - The Philistines disgrace Saul in death.
   - They publish the news in the house of their idols.
   - The severed head and the displayed armor are similar to what was done with Goliath.
1. The men of Jabesh-gilead try to honor Saul as they can, recovering the bodies from the wall and giving them a proper burial.
   - Israel believes they now have no king and are lost.
   - David is nearby, celebrating in Judah, ready to contend with the Philistines for the city, ready to establish his promised eternal royal line.
