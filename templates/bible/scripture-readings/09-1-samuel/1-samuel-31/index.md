1. Israel flees before the Philistines and the slain fall on Mount Gilboa
   ([v. 1](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-1)).
   - The battle is reported in one verse with no drama, for the outcome was fixed the night Samuel
     spoke at Endor ([ch. 28](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-19)).
   - Gilboa is the same ground where the armies mustered in
     [ch. 29](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29), and David is not on it.
1. The Philistines overtake Saul's sons, and Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua are killed
   ([v. 2](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-2)).
   - Jonathan dies beside the father who threw a spear at him
     ([ch. 20](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-20-33)) and in the army of the king he knew God had
     rejected.
   - He is not punished for Saul's sin, but he shares Saul's fate, because a house under judgment
     falls together and the righteous inside it are not exempted from the ruin.
   - The covenant Jonathan made with David in [ch. 20](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-20) now
     binds David to Jonathan's offspring, which is why Mephibosheth exists in the next book.
1. The archers find Saul, and he asks his armor-bearer to run him through
   ([vv. 3](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-3)–[4](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-4)).
   - His stated fear is that the uncircumcised will make sport of him, so his last concern is his
     own shame rather than his people or his God.
   - The armor-bearer will not do it, and the reason given is fear, though the refusal is also the
     last time anyone treats Saul's body as the LORD's anointed.
   - Saul falls on his own sword. The man who spent his reign seizing what God had not given him
     seizes his death as well, and he will not wait for God to end it.
1. The armor-bearer follows him, and Saul, his three sons, his armor-bearer, and his men die together
   ([vv. 5](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-5)–[6](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-6)).
   - Samuel said "tomorrow you and your sons shall be with me," and the sentence lands on the day
     named.
   - The delay between the word in [ch. 13](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-13-14) and the
     execution here is roughly the length of a reign, and the length of the delay says nothing about
     the certainty of the word.
1. The men of Israel abandon their towns across the valley and the Jordan, and the Philistines move in
   ([v. 7](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-7)).
   - Israel demanded a king so he would go out before them and fight their battles
     ([ch. 8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-8-20)), and the book closes with that king dead and
     the land occupied.
   - The people got exactly what they asked for, and the asking was the sin.
1. The Philistines cut off Saul's head, strip his armor into the temple of Ashtoreth, and fasten his
   body to the wall of Beth-shan
   ([vv. 8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-8)–[10](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-10)).
   - They publish the news in the house of their idols, which makes his corpse an argument for Dagon,
     and this is precisely the humiliation he killed himself to avoid.
   - The severed head and the displayed armor are the mirror of
     [ch. 17](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-17), where Israel's champion did this to theirs.
     Saul was the man who should have fought Goliath and did not.
1. The men of Jabesh-gilead march all night, take the bodies down, burn them, and bury the bones under
   the tamarisk, then fast seven days
   ([vv. 11](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-11)–[13](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-31-13)).
   - Saul's first act as king was rescuing that town from Nahash the Ammonite
     ([ch. 11](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-11)), and they are the only ones left who remember
     him at his best.
   - Their valor is real and their debt is old, and it is the single act of steadfast love in the
     chapter.
   - The book of 1 Samuel ends with a week of fasting over a burned body and no successor named,
     while the man God chose is three days' ride south handing out mutton in Judah.
