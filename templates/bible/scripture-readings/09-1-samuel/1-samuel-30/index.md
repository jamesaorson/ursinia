1. David returns to Ziklag on the third day and finds it burned and every wife and child carried off
   ([vv. 1](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-1)–[3](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-3)).
   - The raiders are Amalekites, the nation Saul was commanded to devote to destruction in
     [ch. 15](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-15) and spared for the sake of plunder.
   - Saul's disobedience has outlived his reign, and the man who will inherit his throne now pays
     part of the bill for it.
   - The town David took as a base for sixteen months of lying to Achish
     ([ch. 27](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-27)) is the town he loses.
1. David and his men weep until they have no strength left to weep, and the men speak of stoning him
   ([vv. 4](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-4)–[6](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-6)).
   - The grief is real and the anger is understandable, for it was David's arrangement with the
     Philistines that left the town undefended.
   - David strengthened himself in the LORD his God, which is set down as an act, not a mood, and it
     is the exact hinge on which this chapter turns away from
     [ch. 28](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28).
   - Saul in extremity went to Endor by night, and David in extremity goes to God. The two chapters
     are placed near each other so the reader cannot miss it.
1. David calls for Abiathar and the ephod and inquires of the LORD
   ([vv. 7](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-7)–[8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-8)).
   - Abiathar is with David because he is the survivor of the priests Saul slaughtered at Nob
     ([ch. 22](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-22)), so the means of inquiry Saul destroyed is
     standing in David's camp.
   - Saul asked and heard nothing; David asks and is answered plainly: pursue, overtake, rescue all.
   - This is the first time in many chapters that David is recorded seeking the LORD before acting,
     and it comes after the worst consequence of acting without him.
1. Two hundred men are too exhausted to cross the Brook Besor and stay with the baggage
   ([vv. 9](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-9)–[10](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-10)).
   - A third of the force drops out before the battle, which matters later when the spoil is divided.
1. An abandoned Egyptian slave, fed and revived, leads David to the raiding party
   ([vv. 11](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-11)–[15](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-15)).
   - His master left him to die when he fell sick three days earlier, and that cruelty is the reason
     he is available to David at all.
   - The Amalekites' contempt for a slave is what undoes them, and the LORD's providence runs through
     an unnamed foreigner nobody thought worth keeping.
   - David feeds him before questioning him, and only then does the man ask for an oath.
1. David attacks from twilight to the evening of the next day and recovers everything
   ([vv. 16](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-16)–[20](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-20)).
   - The Amalekites are found eating, drinking, and dancing over the plunder, which is how the text
     usually describes people immediately before judgment falls.
   - Nothing was missing, small or great, and the promise of
     [v. 8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-8) is fulfilled to the word.
   - David also takes the flocks and herds the raiders had gathered elsewhere, so he comes home with
     more than he lost, though the chapter never presents that as the point.
1. David rules that the men who stayed with the baggage share equally with the men who fought
   ([vv. 21](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-21)–[25](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-25)).
   - His own men call the two hundred wicked and worthless and would return their wives and children
     and nothing else, which is a claim of ownership over what God gave.
   - David's answer grounds the ruling in the giver: the LORD preserved them and delivered the
     raiders, so the spoil was never the fighters' to allocate.
   - The ruling becomes a statute and an ordinance in Israel from that day forward, and it is worth
     noting that David is legislating equity while still a fugitive with no throne.
1. David sends portions of the spoil to the elders of Judah in the towns where he had roamed
   ([vv. 26](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-26)–[31](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-31)).
   - Sixteen months in Philistine territory left Judah wondering whose side he was on, and this
     answers the question in goods rather than words.
   - It is generosity and it is also politics, and the text is content to let both stand without
     comment.
   - Hebron is on the list, and Hebron is where Judah will anoint him king in
     [2 Samuel 2](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#2-samuel-2-4).
