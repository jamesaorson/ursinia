1. David returns to Ziklag and finds it raided.
   - The raiders are Amalekites, the nation Saul was commanded to devote to destruction in
     [ch. 15](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-15) and spared.
   - Saul's disobedience has harmed the next king.
1. David and his men weep.
   - The men consider stoning David.
   - David remembers the promises of the Lord and is strengthened by those promises, deciding to submit to the Lord for guidance instead. 
     - David calls for Abiathar and the ephod and inquires of the LORD ([vv. 7](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-7)–[8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-8)).
   - Saul asked and heard nothing, but David asks and is answered plainly by the Lord that the Lord will deliver the Amalekites to David for destruction.
1. Two hundred men are too exhausted to cross the Brook Besor and stay with the baggage
   ([vv. 9](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-9)–[10](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-10)).
   - A third of the force drops out before the battle, which matters later when the spoil is divided.
1. An abandoned Egyptian slave, fed and revived, leads David to the raiding party
   ([vv. 11](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-11)–[15](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-15)).
   - The Amalekites' contempt for a slave is what undoes them and is a vehicle for God’s providence.
1. David attacks and recovers everything
   ([vv. 16](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-16)–[20](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-20)).
   - Nothing was missing, and the promise of
     [v. 8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-8) is fulfilled to the word.
1. David rules that the men who stayed with the baggage share equally with the men who fought
   ([vv. 21](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-21)–[25](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-25)).
   - His men complain that the two hundred were wicked and worthless, and they would only return their wives and children
     and nothing else.
   - David declares instead a new ordinance on how to split the spoil, asserting his kingly authority.
1. David sends portions of the spoil to the elders of Judah in the towns where he had roamed
   ([vv. 26](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-26)–[31](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-30-31)).
   - This shows those of Judah that his loyalty is to them.
   - Hebron is on the list that he gives to, and Hebron is where Judah will anoint him king.
