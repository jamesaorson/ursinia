1. David returns to Jonathan in desperation: "there is but a step between me and death" (vv. 1–3).
   - Jonathan still believes Saul would not move against David without telling him first.
   - David corrects him: Saul is concealing his murderous intent precisely because he knows Jonathan loves David.
   - David appeals to the covenant of the LORD already between them (v. 8), asking Jonathan to slay him himself rather than hand him to Saul.
1. Jonathan devises the new-moon test to reveal his father's intent (vv. 4–23).
   - David will be absent from the feast. Jonathan will gauge Saul's response to explain David's absence.
   - If Saul receives the excuse well, David is safe. If Saul is enraged, his murderous resolve is confirmed.
   - The signal will be delivered by arrow: if the arrows are called "on this side of thee," come; if "beyond thee," flee.
   - Jonathan seals the plan with a covenant oath before the LORD, asking David to show kindness to his house when the LORD has cut off all of David's enemies (vv. 14–16).
     - Jonathan is not asking for David's favor if David wins; he is speaking as one who already knows David will be king.
     - This covenant extends to Jonathan's offspring, not merely to Jonathan himself.
1. At the feast, Saul's silence on the first day breaks into rage on the second (vv. 24–34).
   - Saul at first assumes David is ceremonially unclean — a charitable reading of his absence.
   - On the second day Saul interrogates Jonathan, and Jonathan gives the agreed-upon excuse.
   - Saul's response is unmasked fury: he insults Jonathan's mother, declares that Jonathan's kingdom cannot stand as long as David lives, and commands Jonathan to bring David to him to die (vv. 30–31).
   - When Jonathan asks what David has done to deserve death, Saul hurls his spear at Jonathan — confirming beyond doubt that it is determined against David (v. 33).
   - Jonathan leaves the table in fierce anger and grief, both for David's sake and because of the shame his father has done (v. 34).
1. Jonathan sends the signal and the covenant farewell (vv. 35–42).
   - Jonathan shoots the arrow beyond the lad, delivering the message: go, flee.
   - When the lad returns to the city, David comes out and prostrates himself before Jonathan three times; they weep together until David exceeds in weeping (v. 41).
   - Jonathan's final word to David invokes the LORD's covenant: "The LORD be between me and thee, and between my seed and thy seed for ever" (v. 42).
1. Jonathan is the rightful heir who surrenders the kingdom for the LORD's anointed.
   - He strips himself of his royal regalia in chapter 18, and here he strips himself of his father, his table, his future — for David's sake.
   - His love for David is covenantal, not merely sentimental; it is ordered by what he sees the LORD doing.
   - The LORD's anointed advances not through conquest or scheming but through the willing sacrifice of those who recognize him.
