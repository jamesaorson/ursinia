1. David's deception of Achish has trapped him, for Achish now expects David to march against Israel
   ([vv. 1](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-1)–[2](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-2)).
   - David's answer is deliberately ambiguous, and it can be read as either loyalty or threat.
   - The lie of [ch. 27](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-27) has come due, and David has no clean way out of it.
1. The narrator pauses to remind us that Samuel is dead and that Saul had expelled the mediums
   ([v. 3](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-3)).
   - Both notes exist to set up what follows, for Saul is about to seek the one prophet he cannot
     reach, by the one means he himself outlawed.
   - Saul's earlier obedience here was outward reform without a changed heart, and it does not hold under pressure.
1. Saul sees the Philistine army, is afraid, and inquires of the LORD, but receives no answer by dreams, Urim, or prophets
   ([vv. 5](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-5)–[6](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-6)).
   - All three ordinary channels are named and all three are shut.
   - The silence is not God's absence but God's verdict, already pronounced in
     [ch. 15](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-15).
   - Saul is not seeking the LORD so much as an outcome, and when the LORD does not supply it,
     he goes elsewhere.
1. Saul disguises himself and goes by night to the medium at Endor
   ([vv. 7](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-7)–[8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-8)).
   - The disguise and the darkness are the confession, for he knows this is forbidden
     (Deuteronomy 18:10–12; Leviticus 19:31).
   - He swears by the LORD's life that no punishment will come upon her
     ([v. 10](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-10)), invoking the God whose law he is breaking in the same breath.
1. Samuel appears, and the woman screams
   ([vv. 12](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-12)–[14](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-14)).
   - Her terror is the point, for she did not expect this to work, and her cry exposes the trade as fraud.
   - The text says plainly that it was Samuel, and that God, not her craft, produced him.
     Necromancy did not summon a prophet, but God overruled a forbidden act to deliver a true word of judgment.
1. Samuel's message adds nothing new, but it fixes the date
   ([vv. 16](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-16)–[19](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-19)).
   - The kingdom torn away and given to David is the same sentence from
     [ch. 15](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-15-28), and the cause named is the same: Amalek.
   - What is new is "tomorrow," for Saul went seeking guidance and received an execution notice.
1. Saul falls to the ground, and the chapter ends with a last meal served by a witch
   ([vv. 20](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-20)–[25](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28-25)).
   - He rises, eats, and walks out into the night to die.
   - There is no repentance anywhere in the chapter, only fear of consequence, which is not the same thing.
