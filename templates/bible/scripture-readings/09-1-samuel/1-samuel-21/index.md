1. David arrives at Nob alone, without weapons or provisions, and deceives the priest Ahimelech (vv. 1–2).
   - Ahimelech is alarmed that David is alone — a king's officer traveling without retinue signals danger.
   - David constructs a lie: a secret royal mission, his men meeting him elsewhere.
   - This deception will have catastrophic consequences. Doeg the Edomite, Saul's chief herdsman, witnesses the entire exchange (v. 7).
1. Ahimelech gives David the consecrated showbread (vv. 3–6).
   - There is no common bread; only the bread of the Presence, reserved for the priests (Lev. 24:9).
   - Ahimelech grants it on the condition of ritual purity, and David affirms it.
   - The LORD permitted the bread to be given to David in his necessity.
   - Jesus cites this very episode in defending his disciples plucking grain on the Sabbath (Matt. 12:3–4), establishing that mercy and human necessity do not violate the law's intent — the law serves life, not the other way around.
1. David takes Goliath's sword from behind the ephod (vv. 8–9).
   - David has nothing — not even his weapons. He asks Ahimelech if there is any sword at hand.
   - The only sword there is Goliath's, stored as a trophy at the sanctuary.
   - The man who won the greatest victory for Israel is now a fugitive whose only weapon is the spoil of that victory.
   - David's response: "There is none like that; give it me."
1. David flees to Achish, king of Gath, and feigns madness to survive (vv. 10–15).
   - Gath is Goliath's city. David flees to the Philistines — the irony is stark.
   - The servants of Achish recognize David immediately and his fame as Israel's champion.
   - David is afraid. He feigns madness, scrabbling at the gate and letting spittle fall on his beard.
   - Achish dismisses him as a lunatic and sends him away.
   - The LORD's anointed, Israel's greatest warrior, is preserved at the lowest point of his flight through humiliation before a pagan king.
1. David's descent in this chapter is total: from a covenant feast with Jonathan to spittle on his beard at a Philistine gate.
   - He has no bread, no sword, no army, no court, no home.
   - He is sustained only by what he receives from the house of God and by his own desperate cunning.
   - Psalm 34 is superscribed to this Gath episode: "I will bless the LORD at all times; his praise shall continually be in my mouth."
     - In the moment of deepest humiliation David's praise does not cease.
     - The LORD is not absent from Nob or Gath; he is present in the bread, in the sword, and in the madman's survival.
