1. The armies muster, and David and his men are marching in the rear with Achish, against Israel
   ([vv. 1](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-1)–[2](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-2)).
   - This is the end of the road David chose in [ch. 27](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-27),
     and he is now positioned to fight the LORD's people alongside the LORD's enemies.
   - The narrative places this alongside [ch. 28](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-28)
     deliberately, for Saul is consulting a medium on one side of the field while David is
     marching with the Philistines on the other.
1. The Philistine commanders object, and they will not have David in the line
   ([vv. 3](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-3)–[5](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-5)).
   - Achish vouches for David and says he has found no fault in him, which is only true because David has lied to him well.
   - The commanders quote the same song the women of Israel sang in
     [ch. 18](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-18-7), and their suspicion is more accurate
     than Achish's trust.
   - Their fear is that David will turn on them mid-battle to buy back Saul's favor with Philistine heads
     ([v. 4](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-4)).
1. Achish dismisses David, swearing by the LORD and calling him blameless as an angel of God
   ([vv. 6](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-6)–[9](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-9)).
   - A pagan king swears by the God of Israel over a man who has deceived him for sixteen months.
   - The irony is not that Achish is wrong about David's conduct toward him, but that he is wrong
     about everything underneath it.
1. David protests his dismissal
   ([v. 8](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-8)).
   - The protest is as ambiguous as everything he has said to Achish, for "the enemies of my lord
     the king" can be read either way.
   - The text does not tell us what David intended to do had he reached the battle, and that
     silence is itself an indictment, for a man walking with the LORD would not have been standing
     there at all.
1. David leaves at first light, and the Philistines go up to Jezreel
   ([v. 11](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-29-11)).
   - David is extracted from an impossible position by pagan suspicion, and the LORD is never mentioned as acting.
   - This is providence working through the hostility of unbelievers, and the deliverance is real
     even though David neither asked for it nor deserved it.
   - It also clears him of any hand in Saul's death, which matters greatly for what comes next.
