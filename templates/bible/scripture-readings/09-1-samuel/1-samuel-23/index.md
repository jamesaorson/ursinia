1. David inquires of the LORD twice before going to fight the Philistines at Keilah (vv. 1–5).
   - His men are afraid. He inquires a second time, and the LORD confirms the command.
   - David saves an Israelite city while Saul is trying to kill him. The fugitive is doing the king's job.
   - Abiathar brought the ephod when he fled to David (v. 6). The means of divine inquiry now reside with the LORD's anointed, not with Saul's court.
1. Saul misreads providence (v. 7).
   - He hears David has entered a walled city and concludes: "God has delivered him into my hand, for he has trapped himself."
   - This is Saul's theology in a sentence — providence is a tool for reading your own desires back onto God. He has not inquired of the LORD in years. He does not now.
1. David inquires of the LORD again at Keilah: will the citizens hand him over? Will Saul come down? (vv. 9–12).
   - Both answers are yes.
   - The men of Keilah would deliver the man who just rescued them from the Philistines.
   - David flees. He does not wait to see whether gratitude would win out; he trusts the word of the LORD over any human loyalty.
1. David and his six hundred men move continuously through the wilderness (vv. 13–14).
   - "Day after day Saul searched for him, but God would not deliver David into his hand."
   - That is the chapter's verdict rendered early. Everything that follows illustrates it.
1. Jonathan comes to David in Horesh — his last recorded visit — and strengthens his hand in God (vv. 15–18).
   - "Do not be afraid, for my father Saul will never lay a hand on you. And you will be king over Israel, and I will be your second-in-command. Even my father Saul knows this is true."
   - Jonathan names the reality his father will not name. He has accepted his own displacement.
   - They renew the covenant before the LORD. Then Jonathan goes home. They will not see each other again.
1. The Ziphites, from within Judah's own territory, betray David's location to Saul (vv. 19–23).
   - The LORD's anointed is rejected even in his own tribal land.
   - Saul blesses the Ziphites "by the LORD" for their treachery. He still speaks the name while doing the opposite of the LORD's will.
1. Saul closes in and nearly catches David on opposite sides of the same mountain (vv. 24–26).
   - The tension is physical and immediate: Saul's men are encircling, David is hurrying.
1. A messenger arrives: the Philistines have raided the land. Saul breaks off the pursuit (vv. 27–29).
   - The LORD uses Saul's enemies to rescue David. No angel, no miracle — just a raid, at the right moment.
   - The place is named Sela-hammahlekoth, "Rock of Parting." Providence is given a monument in the name of the place itself.
   - David retreats to En-gedi, and the chapter closes the same way it opened: the LORD did not deliver David into Saul's hand.
