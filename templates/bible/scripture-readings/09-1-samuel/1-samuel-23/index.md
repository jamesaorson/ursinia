1. David inquires of the LORD twice before going to fight the Philistines at Keilah
   ([vv. 1](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23-1)–[5](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23-5)).
   - David saves an Israelite city while Saul is trying to kill him, further distinguishing the Lord’s anointed from the
     current holder of the throne.
   - Abiathar brought the ephod when he fled to David ([v. 6](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23-6)). The means of
     divine inquiry reside with the LORD's anointed David, not with Saul.
1. Saul misreads providence ([v. 7](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23-7)).
   - He hears David has entered a walled city and concludes: "God has delivered him into my hand, for he has trapped himself."
   - Saul’s understanding of providence is as a tool for reading your own desires back onto God.
1. David inquires of the LORD again at Keilah
   ([vv. 9](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23-9)–[12](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23-12)).
   - David considers the will of the LORD in all he is doing, not trusting in his own judgment, but rather in the will
     and judgment of God.
1. Jonathan comes to David to encourage him that his reign is secure
   ([v. 17](/bible/versions/bsb/text/#1-samuel-23-17)).
   - The LORD has continuously delivered David from Saul’s hand, and even provided for him loyal followers before he has
     claimed the throne.
   - The LORD’s promises are not subject to the actions of man.
