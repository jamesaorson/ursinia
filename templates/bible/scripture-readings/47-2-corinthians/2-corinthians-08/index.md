1. Paul holds up the Macedonian churches as a model of generosity (vv. 1-5)
   1. Their giving was not out of surplus — it came from extreme poverty, and was beyond their means
   1. They gave of their own accord, even begging for the privilege of participating in the collection for the saints in Jerusalem
   1. Crucially: they gave themselves first to the Lord, and then to Paul — generosity flows from prior consecration, not from circumstance
1. Paul urges the Corinthians to excel in this grace as they excel in others (vv. 6-9)
   1. Giving is named among gifts like faith, speech, knowledge, and love — it is a grace to be cultivated, not merely a duty to be discharged
   1. Paul is not commanding but testing the genuineness of their love against the earnestness of others (v. 8)
   1. The supreme argument for generosity is Christ himself (v. 9): though he was rich, he became poor, so that by his poverty we might become rich
      1. This is the logic of the incarnation applied to the wallet
      1. If the Lord of all things gave up everything for us, our surplus held tightly is revealed as ingratitude
1. Paul gives practical guidance, not command (vv. 10-15)
   1. The Corinthians had already begun this a year prior; they should now complete what they started, matching their eagerness then with action now
   1. The willingness is what is accepted — giving must be proportionate to what one has, not what one does not have (v. 12)
   1. The goal is equality (v. 14): the Corinthians' abundance now meets Jerusalem's need, and Jerusalem's abundance may later meet theirs
      1. This is an economic application of the solidarity of the body of Christ
      1. Paul quotes Exodus 16:18 — the manna account — as the pattern: each had what was needed, no more and no less
1. Paul commends Titus and his companions as trustworthy stewards of the gift (vv. 16-24)
   1. Titus goes of his own accord, sharing Paul's earnestness for the Corinthians (v. 17)
   1. A second brother, famous among all the churches for his gospel work, accompanies him — Paul is scrupulously transparent about the administration of the funds
   1. Paul takes explicit precautions against any charge of dishonesty (v. 20) — the church's witness in financial integrity is not a peripheral concern
   1. These men are the Corinthians' ambassadors and Christ's glory — receiving them rightly is receiving Paul, and Paul's Lord
