1. Paul opens with a renewed appeal for the Corinthians to make room for him in their hearts (v. 2)
   1. He has wronged no one, corrupted no one, taken advantage of no one — in contrast to the false apostles who have done exactly this
   1. This is not a self-defense but an invitation to restored fellowship — he has already said they are in his heart (6:11)
   1. He speaks not to condemn them, but out of his deep affection — they are together in death and life (v. 3)
1. Even amid his great affliction in Macedonia — conflicts without, fears within — God comforted Paul (vv. 5-7)
   1. Paul is consistent: the same God who comforts the downcast (Isaiah 49) is the God who comforts him here
   1. The instrument of comfort is Titus, whose arrival brings not only his own presence but the report of the Corinthians' longing, mourning, and zeal for Paul
   1. This is a pattern of how God works: through means, through brothers, through the church — not apart from it
1. The "severe letter" caused the Corinthians grief, but Paul does not regret sending it (vv. 8-10)
   1. Godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation, without regret
   1. Worldly grief — the sorrow of being caught, or of consequence only — produces death
   1. The distinction is in the object: godly grief is grief over sin against God; worldly grief is grief over loss of comfort or reputation
1. The fruit of their godly grief was evident (v. 11)
   1. Earnestness, vindication, indignation, fear, longing, zeal, and punishment — all signs that the grief was genuine and operative
   1. Paul's purpose in writing was not merely to address the specific offender or offended party, but that their earnestness for him — and for righteousness — might be revealed before God
1. Paul's joy is complete because Titus's report confirms that his boasting about the Corinthians was not in vain (vv. 13-16)
   1. Titus came away with his spirit refreshed and his affection for the Corinthians increased
   1. The Corinthians received him with fear and trembling — a reverence appropriate to one sent by an apostle
   1. Paul has full confidence in them — the same confidence he has been building toward throughout the letter
