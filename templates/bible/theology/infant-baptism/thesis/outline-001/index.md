## 0. Framing, Method, and Scope

1. **What is actually being argued?** Four candidate theses, in descending strength. Pick one before drafting.
   1. **Strong thesis:** Infant baptism is a corruption — a post-apostolic innovation with no biblical warrant,
      produced by a defective sacramentology and retained by inertia.
      1. Cost: has to survive the Col. 2 / covenant-continuity argument on its own terms, not by dismissing it.
      1. Cost: makes most of the church wrong about a sacrament for 1,200 years, which is a large bill to pay.
      1. Benefit: it is what the 1689 actually implies, and it is the honest Baptist position.
   1. **Moderate thesis:** Infant baptism is a _development_, not an apostolic practice; its historic engine
      (baptismal regeneration + original sin) was rejected at the Reformation, and the covenantal rationale
      later supplied is a reconstruction that does not carry the weight the original mechanism did.
      1. **[CLAIM]** This is the defensible version and probably the paper.
      1. It lets the historical argument do the work without requiring a knockout exegetical blow.
   1. **Narrow thesis (mechanism-only):** Bracket who is right. Trace _how_ a contested minority practice in
      AD 200 became the universal assumed practice by AD 500, and show that the causal chain is pastoral and
      sacramental rather than exegetical.
      1. This is the most novel contribution and the least attackable.
      1. **[Q]** Does James want the history or the polemic? The history is more useful; the polemic is more
         satisfying. They can coexist but only if the history comes first and is not bent.
   1. **Descriptive thesis:** Simply map the four paedobaptist rationales and the Baptist rationale and show
      they are not variants of one argument.
      1. Weakest as a thesis, strongest as a _chapter_. Fold into [Part VI](#vi-the-paedobaptist-distinctives).
1. **Confessional guardrails** (state explicitly in the methodology section).
   1. Scripture is the sole infallible rule. Patristic evidence establishes _what happened_, never _what ought_.
      1. **[TENSION]** But the entire paper's rhetorical force depends on patristic evidence. If history cannot
         norm practice, why does it matter that infant baptism is late? Answer: it matters because paedobaptists
         claim apostolic _succession of practice_. The argument is internal to their position, not to mine.
         Say this out loud early or the paper looks like it is doing tradition-as-authority.
   1. 1689 LBCF ch. 29 (Of Baptism) is the operative standard: baptism is for "those who do actually profess
      repentance towards God, faith in, and obedience to, our Lord Jesus Christ," by immersion.
   1. 1689 ch. 7 and ch. 8 on the covenant of grace — the Baptist covenantal case lives here and must be
      developed, not assumed. Coxe and Owen are the load-bearing sources.
   1. Compare WCF 28 and the Westminster Larger Catechism Q. 166 directly. The 1689's revision of WCF 28 is
      one of its most deliberate divergences and shows exactly where the two traditions part.
   1. **[GAP]** The Baptist Catechism (1695) and An Orthodox Catechism (Collins, 1680) both treat baptism and
      are available locally. Neither is currently cited anywhere in this project.
1. **Genre and audience.** Decide early; it changes everything.
   1. Reformed paedobaptists who assume the Baptist has no covenant theology. This is the hardest and most
      valuable audience.
   1. Baptists who believe the historical case is easier than it is and who repeat Stander without reading Ferguson.
   1. **[Q]** Serving both is possible here, unlike the monarchy paper — the historical spine is neutral enough
      to interest both. But the conclusion cannot be.
1. **What this paper is _not_.**
   1. Not a denial that paedobaptists are Christians or their churches true churches.
   1. Not an argument that infant baptism is the _cause_ of nominalism, Christendom, or state-church abuse —
      that is a correlation the Baptist polemic habitually overstates.
   1. Not a claim that the Fathers were crypto-Baptists. Stander is sometimes read this way and it is not
      what the evidence supports.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Stating the negatives early buys enormous goodwill and prevents the paper from being read
      as sectarian score-settling.
1. **Definitions to fix before Part I.**
   1. _Paedobaptism_ — baptism administered to an infant on the basis of someone else's faith or status.
   1. _Credobaptism / believers' baptism_ — baptism administered on personal profession.
   1. _Baptismal regeneration_ — the view that the rite itself effects the remission of sin and the new birth.
      1. Distinguish carefully from _sacramental efficacy_ more broadly. Lutherans affirm efficacy without
         the ex opere operato mechanism. Conflating them wrecks [Part VI](#vi-the-paedobaptist-distinctives).
   1. _Oikos / household_ — the disputed term. Note in advance that the argument from household baptisms is
      an argument from silence _on both sides_.
   1. _Catechumenate_ — the structured pre-baptismal instruction period. Its rise and collapse is arguably the
      real story of this paper.
      1. **[CLAIM]** The catechumenate is the hinge institution. As long as it existed, baptism presupposed
         profession. Its dissolution, not any council, is what made infant baptism the default.
   1. **[Q]** Is _immersion vs. affusion_ a separate paper or part of this one? Stander treats them together.
      They are logically independent — one could immerse infants (the East does) — but historically entangled.
      Recommend: include as a subsection, not a part, so the mode question does not swallow the subject question.

## I. The New Testament Evidence

Purpose of this part: establish that the NT neither commands nor records infant baptism, while conceding
honestly that it does not explicitly forbid it either. The Baptist case is not won here; it is _set up_ here.

1. **The pattern of the commands.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** Every baptismal command in the NT presupposes a responsive subject.
      1. **[EV]** Matt. 28:19–20 — disciple, baptize, teach. The participial structure ties baptism to discipling.
      1. **[EV]** Mark 16:16 — "believes and is baptized." Note the textual problem with the longer ending;
         do not lean on this one.
      1. **[EV]** Acts 2:38 — "Repent and be baptized." Repentance precedes.
      1. **[EV]** Acts 8:12, 8:36–38 — belief precedes; the Ethiopian's "what prevents me" presupposes capacity.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Every recorded baptism in Acts has a professing subject where the subject is described at all.
      1. **[Q]** How many baptisms in Acts actually describe the subject's faith versus leave it unstated?
         Count them. Do not assert "every" without the count in hand.
1. **The household baptisms.** The paedobaptist's strongest positive NT evidence. Handle it properly.
   1. **[EV]** Acts 16:15 (Lydia), Acts 16:31–34 (Philippian jailer), 1 Cor. 1:16 (Stephanas), Acts 18:8 (Crispus).
   1. **The Baptist reply, and its limits.**
      1. Acts 16:34 — the jailer "rejoiced along with his entire household that he had believed in God."
         The household is described as believing. **[EV]** strongest single counter-text.
      1. 1 Cor. 16:15 — the household of Stephanas "devoted themselves to the service of the saints."
         Infants do not do this.
      1. **[TENSION]** These replies show the households _contained_ believers. They do not show the households
         contained _no_ infants. The Baptist argument here is an inference, not a demonstration. Admit it.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The _oikos_ formula is an argument from silence for both sides, and arguments from silence
      favor neither. The real weight has to come from the commands, not the narratives.
      1. **[EV]** Jeremias, _Infant Baptism in the First Four Centuries_ — argues the oikos formula is a
         technical Jewish household-inclusion term. This is the serious scholarly version of the argument.
      1. **[EV]** Aland, _Did the Early Church Baptize Infants?_ — the direct rebuttal. The Jeremias/Aland
         exchange (1958–1963) is the foundational modern debate and must be read in sequence, not summarized
         from secondary sources.
      1. **[GAP]** Need Jeremias's _reply_ to Aland (_The Origins of Infant Baptism_, 1963), not just the
         first book. The debate's final state is frequently misreported.
1. **The circumcision typology.** Col. 2:11–12 is the covenantal case's exegetical anchor.
   1. **[EV]** Col. 2:11–12 — "circumcision of Christ... having been buried with him in baptism."
   1. **Paedobaptist reading:** baptism replaces circumcision as the covenant sign; the subjects therefore
      carry over; infants of believers receive the sign.
   1. **Baptist reading:** the text ties baptism to the _circumcision made without hands_ — regeneration — and
      to burial and rising _through faith_ (v. 12). The continuity is spiritual, not administrative.
      1. **[CLAIM]** v. 12's "through faith in the powerful working of God" is the decisive clause and is
         routinely underweighted in the paedobaptist reading.
   1. **[TENSION]** The Baptist cannot simultaneously deny that baptism replaces circumcision and affirm that
      baptism is the initiatory sign of the covenant of grace. Some account of what _did_ change is required.
      Owen and Coxe supply it (the Abrahamic covenant is not simply the covenant of grace); develop it.
1. **Rom. 4:11 and the nature of a covenant sign.** Circumcision as "a seal of the righteousness that he had
   by faith" — note that Abraham _believed first_. **[Q]** Does this cut for the Baptist or is it neutralized
   by Isaac receiving the sign at eight days without faith? This is the crux of the whole covenantal dispute.
1. **1 Cor. 7:14 — the "holy" children.**
   1. Paedobaptist: federal holiness of covenant children implies covenant membership implies the sign.
   1. Baptist: the same verse sanctifies the _unbelieving spouse_, who is nobody's candidate for baptism.
      The parallel structure is fatal to the paedobaptist use. **[CLAIM]** This is the Baptist's cleanest
      single exegetical win and should be stated briefly and moved past, not gloated over.

## II. The Pre-Nicene Evidence — Stander's Case

Purpose: the historical spine. This is where Stander & Louw supply the frame and where the paper must be
most careful, because the evidence is genuinely thin in both directions.

1. **Stander & Louw's thesis, stated fairly.**
   1. **[EV]** Stander & Louw, _Baptism in the Early Church_ — pre-Nicene baptism was (a) by immersion, (b) of
      professing candidates, and (c) infant baptism appears as a late, local, and contested variation.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Their method is the strength: they read each patristic text in context rather than harvesting
      proof-phrases. Their weakness is that they are apologists writing history and occasionally the conclusion
      arrives before the evidence.
   1. **[Q]** How is the book actually received in the field? It is published by an Afrikaans Baptist press and
      is not the standard scholarly reference. If the paper leans on it, the paper inherits its reputation.
      **Recommend pairing every Stander citation with Ferguson**, whose _Baptism in the Early Church_ (Eerdmans,
      2009) is the field-standard 900-page treatment and reaches broadly compatible conclusions from a
      non-Baptist-polemical posture.
1. **The genuinely early texts.**
   1. **[EV]** _Didache_ 7 — baptism in running water, cold if possible, pouring permitted if not; preceded by
      fasting _by the candidate_. **[CLAIM]** A fasting candidate is not an infant. This is c. 100 and is the
      earliest church-order evidence.
   1. **[EV]** Justin Martyr, _First Apology_ 61 — "as many as are persuaded and believe... are brought by us."
      Persuasion and belief precede. Also c. 155 and unambiguous.
      1. **[Q]** Justin also mentions those "made disciples from childhood" (_1 Apol._ 15). Does this help the
         paedobaptist? Read the context; it is about lifelong chastity, not baptism. Verify directly.
   1. **[EV]** _Shepherd of Hermas_, _Epistle of Barnabas_ — baptism tied to repentance and forgiveness of
      prior sin. **[GAP]** Need to check whether these actually bear on subjects or only on effects.
1. **Tertullian — the pivot text (c. 200–206).**
   1. **[EV]** _De Baptismo_ 18 — argues for _delay_: "let them come while they are growing up... let them
      become Christians when they have become able to know Christ."
   1. **[CLAIM]** This is the single most important datum in the entire study, and it cuts both ways at once.
      1. It proves infant baptism was being practiced by 200. The Baptist cannot claim it was unknown.
      1. It proves infant baptism was _contested_ by a leading theologian who plainly did not think he was
         innovating. If it were apostolic and universal, this objection is unintelligible.
      1. **[CLAIM]** The burden-shifting move: apostolic practices do not get objected to by orthodox
         teachers as imprudent. Doctrines do not survive that kind of challenge without controversy unless
         they are new.
   1. **[TENSION]** Tertullian also urges delay for the _unmarried_ and _widows_, on the grounds of
      post-baptismal sin. His motive is partly a defective view of post-baptismal forgiveness, not a Baptist
      ecclesiology. Quoting him as a proto-Baptist is a misuse and the paper must say so.
1. **Origen — the strongest paedobaptist patristic claim (c. 240s).**
   1. **[EV]** _Commentary on Romans_ 5.9; _Homilies on Leviticus_ 8.3; _Homilies on Luke_ 14 — infants are
      baptized "for the remission of sins," and the church "received from the apostles the tradition of
      giving baptism even to infants."
   1. **The Baptist replies, ranked by honesty.**
      1. Weak: the Latin texts are Rufinus's translations and may be interpolated. True but overplayed;
         some Greek fragments corroborate. Do not build on this.
      1. Better: Origen's stated _reason_ is the removal of inherited defilement — already the sacramental
         mechanism, not the covenantal one. The practice arrives already attached to its engine.
      1. Best: **[CLAIM]** "Received from the apostles" is what every third-century writer says about every
         practice he inherited. It is a rhetorical form, not a chain of custody. Origen says the same of
         other customs no one regards as apostolic. **[GAP]** Find two or three concrete parallel instances
         or this claim is an assertion.
1. **Cyprian and the Council of Carthage (253).**
   1. **[EV]** Cyprian, _Ep._ 64 — the question put to the council was not _whether_ to baptize infants but
      whether to wait until the _eighth day_ on the analogy of circumcision. The council said no: baptize at once.
   1. **[CLAIM]** This is the moment the practice becomes conciliar and the circumcision analogy appears —
      and note that the analogy is _rejected_ in its timing while the paedobaptism is retained. The reasoning
      is: the mercy of God is denied to no one born.
   1. **[CLAIM]** By 253 the debate's frame has shifted from "should infants be baptized" to "when." That
      shift in fifty years from Tertullian is the mechanism in miniature.
1. **The counter-evidence: delayed baptism of Christian children.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** If infant baptism were universal and apostolic in the fourth century, the children of
      devout Christian parents would all have been baptized as infants. Many demonstrably were not.
      1. **[EV]** Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa, Chrysostom, Jerome, Ambrose, Augustine —
         all born to Christian (often devoutly Christian) families, all baptized as adults.
      1. **[CLAIM]** This is the Baptist's strongest single historical argument and it is empirical, not
         exegetical. Present it as a table with dates of birth and baptism.
      1. **[GAP]** Build that table. It does not exist in the research folder yet and it is the paper's
         most quotable artifact.
   1. **[TENSION]** The delay had a bad motive: fear of post-baptismal sin, which presupposes baptismal
      remission. So the evidence that undercuts paedobaptist antiquity simultaneously confirms that
      sacramental regeneration was already assumed. The Baptist wins the date and loses the theology.
      **This tension is the honest center of Part II and should not be hidden.**
   1. **[EV]** Gregory of Nazianzus, _Oration_ 40 — recommends waiting until about age three, so the child
      can answer something of the mystery. A transitional position, and a revealing one.
1. **Archaeological and epigraphic evidence.**
   1. **[EV]** Baptistery dimensions and depth — do the surviving pre-Constantinian fonts support immersion?
      Dura-Europos (c. 240) is the key site.
   1. **[EV]** Funerary inscriptions recording infant baptism — mostly fourth century and later, and
      concentrated around _emergency_ baptism of dying infants. **[CLAIM]** This is a major clue to the
      mechanism: the earliest hard epigraphic evidence for infant baptism is _deathbed_ infant baptism.
      1. **[GAP]** Need actual inscription citations with dates. Ferguson has them; get the specifics.

## III. The Mechanism — How the Default Shifted

Purpose: the paper's real contribution. Not "who said what" but the causal account. Aim for a multi-factor
explanation, because single-cause history is always wrong.

1. **Factor 1: Baptismal regeneration.** The prior condition without which nothing else works.
   1. **[CLAIM]** If baptism merely _signifies_, delaying it costs nothing. If baptism _effects_ remission,
      delaying it risks everything. Every subsequent factor is downstream of this one.
   1. **[EV]** John 3:5 read as water-baptism from the second century onward; Titus 3:5 "washing of regeneration."
   1. **[Q]** When does regeneration-by-baptism become the settled assumption rather than one reading? It is
      arguably present in Justin. If so, the sacramental engine predates the practice by a century — which
      _weakens_ a simple "the theology was invented to justify the practice" story. Check this carefully;
      the paper's causal direction depends on it.
1. **Factor 2: Original sin and infant guilt.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** Baptismal regeneration alone does not require infant baptism. It requires infant baptism
      only once infants are held to have guilt needing remission. That is the second ingredient.
   1. **[EV]** Cyprian already: infants are baptized for remission "not of their own sins but of another's."
   1. This reaches its final form in [Part IV](#iv-augustine-and-the-lock-in).
1. **Factor 3: Infant mortality and the emergency instinct.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** The most underrated cause. Roman-era infant mortality was perhaps 30% in the first year.
      A theology in which unbaptized infants are lost plus a world in which infants routinely die produces
      emergency baptism. Emergency baptism, normalized, becomes ordinary infant baptism.
      1. **[EV]** The epigraphic pattern noted above: earliest infant baptism evidence is deathbed baptism.
      1. **[CLAIM]** The move from "baptize the dying infant" to "baptize every infant, because any infant
         may be a dying infant" requires no theological innovation at all. It is pure pastoral prudence.
         **This is the mechanism.** If the paper has one original contribution, this is it.
      1. **[GAP]** Need demographic sources for Roman infant mortality. This is a claim about the world,
         not the church, and needs non-theological citation.
1. **Factor 4: Constantine and the collapse of the catechumenate.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** A persecuted church has a natural filter: nobody joins casually. An established church
      does not. The catechumenate was the institutional substitute for persecution, and it could not hold.
   1. **[EV]** Fourth-century catechumenate structure — multi-year, with scrutinies, exorcisms, the
      _traditio symboli_. Cyril of Jerusalem's _Catechetical Lectures_; Ambrose's _De Mysteriis_.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The perverse incentive: the catechumenate's rigor plus post-baptismal penitential severity
      made _remaining a catechumen indefinitely_ the rational strategy. Constantine himself did it.
      The system produced mass deferral, which the church then had to fix.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The fix was to move baptism to the front of life rather than to the end. Once infant
      baptism is universal, the catechumenate has no function and dissolves — leaving confirmation as its
      vestige. **[Q]** Is this the right causal order, or did the catechumenate's decline precede and enable
      the shift? Genuinely uncertain. Find out; the paper's argument bends on it.
1. **Factor 5: The Christianization of the household and the civic frame.**
   1. In a society where the church is coextensive with the population, infant baptism is the sacramental
      form of birth registration. **[Q]** Is this cynical or descriptive? Probably both, and the paper should
      say it descriptively and let the reader draw the cynical conclusion.
   1. **[GAP]** Look for canon law or imperial legislation making baptism a civil marker. If it exists, it
      is a strong data point; if it does not, drop the claim rather than gesturing at it.
1. **Factor 6: The circumcision analogy as _post hoc_ rationalization.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** Note when it appears: Cyprian, 253, and then not as the ground of the practice but as an
      objection about _timing_. It does not become the primary rationale for a thousand years — until Zwingli
      and Calvin need it to be.
   1. **[CLAIM]** A rationale that arrives 1,300 years after the practice is a rationale that did not cause
      the practice. This is a historical claim, not a refutation — but it is a real one.
1. **Synthesis: the causal chain, stated compactly.**
   1. Baptism remits sin → infants have sin → infants die constantly → baptize the dying infant → baptize
      every infant → the catechumenate loses its subject → infant baptism is all that remains → by the
      fifth century no one alive remembers otherwise → Augustine can cite universal practice as proof of
      apostolicity.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Note the last step. The universality that Augustine appeals to as evidence of apostolic
      origin was produced by the preceding four steps, all of which are post-apostolic. **The argument from
      universal consent is circular, and the circle closes in the fifth century.**
   1. **[TENSION]** Do not overstate this into "nobody baptized infants before 200." The honest statement is:
      the practice existed early, locally, and contestedly; the _universality_ is what is late.

## IV. Augustine and the Lock-In

1. **The Pelagian controversy as the decisive moment.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** Before Pelagius, infant baptism was a widespread custom with a fuzzy rationale. After
      Augustine, it is a dogmatic necessity entailed by the doctrine of original sin. The controversy does
      not create the practice; it welds it to the church's soteriology so that removing it becomes heresy.
   1. **[EV]** Augustine, _De Peccatorum Meritis et Remissione_ (412) — infant baptism as proof of original sin.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Note the direction of the argument. Augustine uses the _practice_ to prove the _doctrine_,
      not the reverse. "The church baptizes infants; baptism is for remission of sins; therefore infants have
      sin." This is a remarkable argument and it should be quoted at length.
      1. **[Q]** Does this help or hurt the Baptist? It shows the practice was uncontested enough by 412 to
         function as a premise. That is a concession the paper must make.
1. **The Council of Carthage (418) and the anathema.**
   1. **[EV]** Canon 2 anathematizes denial that newborns are baptized for the remission of original sin.
   1. **[CLAIM]** This is the formal close. From 418 the question is settled by canon, and dissent is not
      a position but a crime. The practice is now self-enforcing.
1. **The cost: the fate of unbaptized infants.**
   1. **[EV]** Augustine's _mitissima poena_ — unbaptized infants suffer the mildest punishment, but suffer.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The medieval invention of _limbus infantium_ is the church trying to soften a conclusion
      its own sacramentology forced. A doctrine requiring a purpose-built theological annex to be bearable
      should be suspected.
   1. **[TENSION]** The Reformed paedobaptist does not hold this and will say so immediately. Correct — and
      that is precisely the argument of [Part VI](#vi-the-paedobaptist-distinctives): they kept the practice
      and discarded the reason.

## V. Medieval Consolidation

Keep this part short. It is connective tissue, not the argument.

1. **Mode: the shift from immersion to affusion.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** Immersion remained the Western norm far later than most Baptists assume — into the
      thirteenth century in many places, and the East never abandoned it.
   1. **[EV]** Aquinas, _ST_ III q. 66 a. 7 — immersion is the "more common" and safer manner, though
      affusion is valid. That is 1270.
   1. **[Q]** What actually drove the change? Candidates: climate in northern Europe, infant health,
      architectural shrinkage of fonts, convenience, clerical control. **[GAP]** Needs real sources; the
      standard Baptist story here is folklore.
1. **Confirmation as the orphaned half of the rite.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** Confirmation exists because infant baptism removed the professing moment from baptism.
      It is the catechumenate's ghost, relocated to the far side of the sacrament.
1. **Scholastic systematization.** Baptism as one of seven sacraments; _ex opere operato_; the character
   indelible. Lombard, Aquinas, Florence, and then Trent Session VII canons on baptism.
   1. **[EV]** Trent, Session VII, canon 13 — anathema against denying infant baptism; canon 14 against
      requiring their later personal ratification.
1. **Medieval dissent.** Petrobrusians, Waldensians, Lollards, Hussites — did any actually practice
   believers' baptism, or is this a Baptist-successionist myth?
   1. **[CLAIM]** Mostly myth, and the paper should say so. The "trail of blood" Baptist-succession thesis
      is bad history and defending it costs the paper its credibility.
   1. **[EV]** Peter of Bruys (d. c. 1131) genuinely did reject infant baptism — an actual instance, not a
      reconstruction. Verify from primary sources (Peter the Venerable's refutation).
   1. **[GAP]** Need a reliable secondary source on medieval anti-paedobaptism that is not written to prove
      Baptist succession.

## VI. The Paedobaptist Distinctives

Purpose: the part James specifically asked for. **The thesis of this part is that there is no such thing as
"the paedobaptist argument."** There are at least four, they rest on incompatible sacramentologies, and they
would not accept each other's grounds.

1. **Roman Catholic: sacramental efficacy and original sin.**
   1. Ground: baptism _causes_ regeneration _ex opere operato_; original sin is really remitted.
   1. Subject rule: any infant, including infants of unbelievers, licitly in danger of death. **[CLAIM]**
      This is the tell. Rome will baptize a dying infant of atheist parents. No covenantal theology can
      justify that; only a causal-efficacy theology can.
   1. **[EV]** CCC 1250, 1257; Trent Session VII; Code of Canon Law 867, 868 §2.
1. **Eastern Orthodox: incorporation and chrismation.**
   1. Ground: baptism as entrance into the life of the church; immediately followed by chrismation _and
      communion_ — infants receive the Eucharist.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The East is the most internally consistent paedobaptist position: if the infant is a full
      member, feed the infant. Western paedobaptists who withhold the Table from baptized infants owe an
      account of the inconsistency.
      1. **[Q]** This is the paedocommunion pressure point. It has split Reformed denominations. How hard
         should the paper press it? It is a strong argument but it is an argument _against the Reformed_
         made _from Orthodox premises_ the Baptist also rejects. Use carefully or it boomerangs.
   1. Also: the East baptizes infants by _immersion_, which decouples the mode question from the subject
      question and is worth noting against sloppy Baptist polemic.
1. **Lutheran: infant faith.**
   1. Ground: the Word attached to the element creates faith; infants _can_ and _do_ believe. Baptism is
      efficacious, but through faith, not mechanically.
   1. **[EV]** Luther, _Large Catechism_ IV; Augsburg Confession IX ("that children are to be baptized, who
      by baptism, being offered to God, are received into God's favour"); Formula of Concord.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Luther's position is often caricatured. He genuinely held that the infant believes — a
      claim the Reformed do not make and Rome does not need. It is a third, distinct rationale.
      1. **[Q]** What is the Lutheran argument that an infant can have _fides_? Luther appeals to John the
         Baptist leaping in the womb. Is there more? Get the actual argument, not the proof-text.
1. **Reformed: the covenant sign.**
   1. Ground: one covenant of grace across both testaments; circumcision and baptism are successive signs
      of the same covenant; children of believers are covenant members and receive the sign.
      **Baptism does not regenerate; it signs and seals a promise.**
   1. **[EV]** Zwingli against the Anabaptists (1525) — the first fully covenantal defense, and it is
      _reactive_. Calvin, _Institutes_ IV.xvi. Westminster Confession 28; WLC 166.
   1. **[CLAIM]** This is the crucial historical observation of the whole paper: **the covenantal argument
      is sixteenth-century, and it was constructed under Anabaptist pressure after the Reformers had
      already demolished the sacramental machinery that originally produced the practice.**
      1. Zwingli in particular had to build it from nothing — his own sacramental theology gave him no
         other resource.
      1. **[Q]** Is this too clever? The Reformed will reply that the covenantal argument is exegetical and
         its date is irrelevant. That reply is _fair_. The counter is that they cannot then also claim the
         Fathers as witnesses to their practice, since the Fathers held it for reasons the Reformed deny.
         **[CLAIM]** The paedobaptist cannot have both antiquity and the covenant argument. Make him choose.
   1. **[TENSION]** The Reformed position is also the one with the least to lose. If baptism only signs,
      then misapplying it to a non-elect infant is an error of administration, not a false gospel. The
      Baptist polemic that treats Reformed paedobaptism as a soul-endangering corruption is aiming at Rome
      and hitting the wrong target.
1. **Anglican: the hybrid, and why it matters.**
   1. **[EV]** Thirty-Nine Articles XXVII; the 1662 BCP baptismal office — "seeing now, dearly beloved
      brethren, that this child is regenerate."
   1. **[CLAIM]** The BCP language is frankly regenerationist while Article XXVII is compatible with the
      Reformed sign-view. That unresolved tension produced the Gorham controversy (1847–50) and is a live
      fault line to this day. It is the clearest demonstration that the two rationales are genuinely
      different theologies wearing one rite.
1. **Comparative table** (build this; it is the deliverable of Part VI).
   1. Columns: tradition | what baptism _does_ | why the infant qualifies | who may be baptized | relation
      to the Table | what happens if unbaptized.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Once tabulated, the disagreement is visible at a glance. The traditions agree on the act
      and on almost nothing else.
   1. **[Q]** Should Presbyterian and Continental Reformed be split? Probably not — but note the Dutch
      "presumptive regeneration" strand (Kuyper) as a genuine variant that drifts toward the Lutheran side.

## VII. The Return of Believers' Baptism

1. **Zurich, January 1525 — the first rebaptism.**
   1. **[EV]** Grebel, Manz, Blaurock; Grebel's letter to Müntzer (1524) already argues against paedobaptism.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The trigger was _sola scriptura_ applied consistently. The Anabaptists were not importing
      an exotic principle; they were running Zwingli's own hermeneutic past the point Zwingli would stop.
      1. **[EV]** Zwingli reportedly conceded in 1523 that children should not be baptized until instructed,
         then reversed. **[GAP]** Verify this; it is widely repeated and may be polemical embellishment.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The Reformers' resistance was substantially _political_, not only theological. Infant
      baptism was the sacramental basis of the corpus christianum; credobaptism dissolves the identity of
      church and civil society. Zwingli understood this immediately.
      1. **[EV]** The Zurich council's 1526 decree of death by drowning for rebaptism. The joke was
         deliberate and tells you the stakes were civic order.
      1. **[EV]** The revived Justinianic law against rebaptism (Code I.6.2) used at Speyer, 1529.
1. **Schleitheim (1527) and the Anabaptist settlement.**
   1. Article 1 on baptism: to those taught repentance, amendment of life, and who walk in the resurrection.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Note what else Schleitheim contains — separation, the ban, the sword, the oath. Credobaptism
      arrived bundled with a sectarian ecclesiology, and that bundling is why it was fought so hard and why
      later Baptists had to work to detach the two.
   1. **[Q]** Is the bundling accidental or entailed? The Baptist answer must be "accidental," but Münster
      (1534–35) is the permanent counter-example the tradition's opponents will always cite. Address Münster
      head-on rather than leaving it to a footnote.
1. **The English line — and its discontinuity from the Continent.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** English Baptists are not descendants of Swiss Anabaptists. The General Baptists (Smyth
      and Helwys, Amsterdam, 1609) had Mennonite contact; the Particular Baptists (Spilsbury, JLJ church,
      1638–41) emerged from English Separatism independently. **The two English Baptist streams have
      different origins and different theologies, and conflating them is a common error.**
   1. **[EV]** Smyth's se-baptism (1609) and his subsequent move toward the Waterlanders.
   1. **[EV]** The 1644 First London Confession — and its Art. 40 recovering _immersion_, which even the
      early English Baptists had initially not practiced. **[CLAIM]** Striking: believers' baptism was
      recovered before immersion was. The subject question and the mode question were solved separately,
      about fifteen years apart.
1. **The 1689 and the covenantal answer.**
   1. **[CLAIM]** The Particular Baptists did not reject covenant theology; they revised it. The Abrahamic
      covenant is not simply the covenant of grace; the new covenant has no mixed membership; therefore the
      sign belongs only to the professing.
   1. **[EV]** Nehemiah Coxe, _A Discourse of the Covenants_ (1681); John Owen on Heb. 8 (whom Coxe defers to).
   1. **[EV]** 1689 ch. 29 alongside WCF 28 — a deliberate, minimal, surgical revision. Diff them line by line
      in an appendix. **[GAP]** Build that diff; it does not exist yet and it is cheap to produce.
   1. **[CLAIM]** This is the real answer to "Baptists have no covenant theology." The 1689 is a covenantal
      document that reaches a credobaptist conclusion _from_ covenant theology, not despite it.
1. **Why it spread: from persecuted sect to global majority.**
   1. Toleration (1689), the Evangelical Revival, Carey and the modern missions movement, the American
      frontier, the Southern Baptist Convention, twentieth-century Pentecostal and non-denominational growth.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Credobaptism's growth correlates with religious disestablishment. Where the church is not
      the nation, infant baptism loses its civic function and its chief practical support. **[Q]** Is this
      causal or coincidental? Test against counter-examples — Latin America, where growth happened under a
      Catholic cultural establishment.
   1. **[GAP]** Need actual global numbers. Roughly: Baptist + Pentecostal + restorationist credobaptist
      bodies against Catholic + Orthodox + Lutheran + Reformed + Anglican. Get real figures from a
      demographic source (Pew, WCD), not from denominational self-reporting.

## VIII. Objections to the Baptist Case

Give these full strength. A paper that only refutes weak versions is worthless.

1. **"The covenant of grace is one; the sign goes to children in both administrations."**
   1. The strongest objection. Answer from Jer. 31:31–34 / Heb. 8 — the new covenant's members all know the
      Lord. Owen's exposition is the anchor.
   1. **[TENSION]** The Baptist must explain what covenant status the children of believers _do_ have, or
      the position looks like it has flattened the family into individualism. 1 Cor. 7:14 has to mean
      something. **[Q]** Is there a Baptist doctrine of covenant children? Some say yes (household
      discipleship, the child's standing under the means of grace). Develop it or concede the point.
1. **"The silence of the NT cuts your way only if you assume discontinuity."**
   1. A Jewish audience told their children were now excluded from the covenant sign would have rioted.
      The NT's silence on that controversy is evidence that no such exclusion was perceived.
   1. **[CLAIM]** This is a genuinely strong argument and the Baptist reply — that the NT _does_ record
      exactly this kind of controversy over circumcision and records no parallel over infants — is good but
      not decisive. Say so.
1. **"1,500 years of the church cannot have been wrong about a sacrament."**
   1. Reply: the same argument defends the Mass, prayers to saints, and the papacy. But note that the reply
      proves too much if pressed carelessly — it cannot be used to make antiquity worthless, since the paper
      spends Part II relying on antiquity.
   1. **[CLAIM]** The correct form: antiquity is evidence, not authority. Part II argues the evidence
      _itself_ points the other way, which is a different claim from dismissing evidence.
1. **"Stander is an apologist, not a historian."** Partly fair. Mitigate by pairing with Ferguson throughout.
1. **"Credobaptism produces its own errors."** Decisional regeneration, repeated baptisms, the baptism of
   very young children on shallow profession, and the loss of any objective covenant sign.
   1. **[CLAIM]** Concede this. Modern Baptist practice — baptizing five-year-olds after a walk down an
      aisle — is not what the 1689 describes, and it is a practical concession to exactly the anxieties
      that produced paedobaptism. **This is the most uncomfortable point in the paper and therefore the
      most important one to include.**

## IX. Application and Open Problems

1. **What follows practically for a 1689 church?** Baptism of professing believers by immersion; no infant
   dedication that functionally imitates baptism; a real account of how covenant children are raised.
1. **[Q]** How should a Baptist church receive members baptized as infants who are unwilling to be
   rebaptized? This is live and divides Reformed Baptists — Bunyan against Kiffin, and the argument has
   never been settled.
1. **[Q]** Does the historical argument actually persuade anyone, or does everyone resolve this exegetically
   and use history as ornament? Worth being honest about in the conclusion.
1. **[GAP]** The paper needs an explicit statement of what would falsify its thesis. Candidate: a securely
   dated, uncontested pre-150 text describing the baptism of an infant. If that existed, Part II collapses.
   Saying so up front is the strongest possible signal of good faith.
